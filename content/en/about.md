# Bonus1337

My name is Maciej. In cybersecurity, I go by the nickname **Bonus1337**.

This Field Manual was created because of a very simple problem: I was learning from labs, courses, books, CTFs, Discord notes, my own mistakes, and dozens of scattered sources. At some point, I realized that if I did not start organizing all of it, I would keep coming back to the same questions from zero.

So I built the kind of place I wish I had found at the beginning.

This is not a course.  
This is not “the ultimate truth”.  
This is not a collection of magic commands.

This is my practical manual for learning security — written in a way that helps understand the process, not just copy payloads.

---

## Who I am

I am someone who learns cybersecurity through practice, analyzing mistakes, and building my own note-taking system.

I have passed **eJPT** and **ICCA** from INE, and before that I went through an intensive learning path around Sekurak Academy Hacker. I am mostly drawn toward offensive security: web exploitation, recon, network fundamentals, Linux, application logic, and real pentesting thinking.

But the longer I stay in this world, the more I see one thing:

a good pentester is not the person who knows the most payloads.

A good pentester understands the application, data, permissions, network, limitations, business impact, and can explain the problem in a way that helps the other side actually know what needs to be fixed.

That is why this manual is not only about attacking. It is also about reporting, process, blue team, cloud, law, certifications, tools, and everything else that builds a technical backbone in cybersecurity.

---

## How I think about security

**Process over payloads.**

Recon, enumeration, exploitation, post-exploitation, report. Without a process, even the best tool becomes random clicking. With a process, even a simple request in Burp can reveal a real vulnerability.

**Understanding over memorization.**

I do not just want to know that a command works. I want to know what it sends, how the server responds, what that means, and where the next hypothesis may appear.

**The report is part of the attack.**

A vulnerability without context is just a curiosity. Only a good description, impact, PoC, scope, reproduction conditions, and recommendation make a finding valuable.

**Blue Team makes Red Team stronger.**

To attack well, you need to understand what is being protected, how detection works, where logs are created, what the SOC sees, and why certain mistakes turn into incidents.

**It is not about being a “hacker”. It is about thinking like an operator.**

Plan. Test hypotheses. Verify. Document. Do not guess.

## Why this Field Manual exists

This manual is my attempt to turn the chaos of learning into something useful.

Here you will find, among other things:

- notes on web security and application vulnerabilities,
- write-ups from labs and CTFs,
- materials for eJPT, ICCA, and other certification paths,
- practical checklists for tools such as Burp Suite, Nmap, and Wazuh,
- thoughts on reporting, career, law, and learning methods,
- technical materials written in a way that you can actually use.

I am mainly writing this for my past self — the person who really wanted to get into cyber, but often got lost in the amount of acronyms, tools, and advice without context.

If you are at a similar stage, this manual may save you some chaos.
